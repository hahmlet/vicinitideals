§ 19.310.6. Design Standards.

A.

Definitions.

1.

New Construction.

For the purposes of this subsection, new construction means new structures or development on a site that was previously undeveloped or from which previously existing structures have been demolished.

2.

Major Exterior Alterations.

Major exterior alterations include any of the following:

a.

Alterations that do not qualify as "exterior maintenance and repair" or "minor exterior alterations."

b.

Demolition or replacement of more than 50% of the surface area of any exterior wall.
For the purposes of this subsection, a wall is considered the entire wall plane if the change in wall plane is less than 24 in.

c.

Additions that increase floor area by more than 75% of the existing floor area, or demolition or replacement of 50% or more of the existing floor area.

3.

Minor Exterior Alterations.

Minor exterior alterations include, but are not limited to, the application or installation of finish building treatments, including windows and other glazing, doors, lintels, copings, awnings, other vertical and horizontal projections, and exterior sheathing and wall cladding materials.

4.

Exterior Maintenance and Repair.

Exterior maintenance and repair includes refurbishing, painting, and weatherproofing of deteriorated materials, and in-kind restoration or replacement of damaged materials.
Exterior maintenance and repair does not include replacement of materials due to obsolescence or replacement undertaken as part of a minor or major exterior renovation, as defined below.

5.

Major Interior Alterations.

Major interior alterations are interior alterations which include demolition or replacement of 50% or more of the existing floor area.

6.

Change in Use.

For the purposes of this subsection, change in use means a change in the primary use from one use to another.

B.

Applicability of Standards.

The site design standards contained in this subsection apply to all properties in the BI Zone.
The building design standards in this subsection apply to the street-facing facades of buildings when the closest wall of the street-facing facade is within 50 ft of a front or street-side lot line.
Existing buildings that do not comply with the site or building design standards may continue and may be modified in accordance with this subsection.

Except as provided for major interior alterations, applicable standards only apply to the proposed modification and not to the nonmodified portion of the existing building.
Exterior maintenance and repair, minor exterior alterations, and changes in use are exempt from these standards.
Subsection 19.310.6A defines new construction, exterior maintenance and repair, and major/minor exterior and interior alterations, and changes in use for the purposes of this subsection.

1.

New Construction.

All design elements apply to the construction of new primary structures.
New accessory structures are exempt from the Access and Main Entrance element in Table 19.310.6.D.1 but all other design elements apply.

2.

Major Exterior Alterations.

All design elements apply to major exterior alterations.
However, major exterior alterations that do not add or modify building entrances are exempt from the Access and Main Entrance element in Table 19.310.6.D.1.

3.

Major Interior Alterations.

Major interior alterations are exempt from the Orientation of Service Activity element in Table 19.310.6.D.2.
All other design elements apply.

C.

Review Process.

An applicant may choose between two review processes available for review of development in the BI zone: objective and discretionary.
Regardless of the review process, the applicant must demonstrate how the applicable standards or guidelines are being met.
Applicants can address design review requirements through a combination of satisfying certain design standards and, in instances where they elect not to utilize design standards, satisfying the applicable design guideline instead.
In such a case, the project will be reviewed using the Type II Development Review process outlined in Subsection 19.310.6C.2.

1.

Objective process: uses clear objective standards that do not require the use of discretionary decision-making.
Developments reviewed against these standards will be processed with Type I Development Review per Subsection 19.906.2A.1.

2.

Discretionary process: uses design guidelines that are more discretionary in nature and are intended to provide the applicant with more design flexibility.
Developments reviewed against these guidelines will be processed with Type II Development Review per Subsection 19.906.2B.1.

D.

Building and Site Design Guidelines and Standards.

Applicable guidelines and standards for the BI zone are located in Table 19.310.6.D.
These standards should not be interpreted as requiring a specific architectural style.
Table 19.310.6.D.1 specifies the primary, secondary, and prohibited material types referenced in Table 19.310.6.D.

1. Pedestrian Experience and Access
Arrange site to provide for safe pedestrian access and connections as well as weather protection.
All buildings must have at least one primary building entrance (e.g., dwelling entrance, customer entrance, tenant entrance, lobby entrance, or breezeway/courtyard entrance) facing an adjoining street (i.e., within 45 degrees of the street property line).
If the building entrance is turned more than 45 degrees from the street (e.g., front door is on a side wall), the primary entrance must not be more than 40 ft from a public sidewalk.
In all cases, a walkway must connect the primary entrance to the sidewalk.
In cases where there is no sidewalk within the adjacent right of way (ROW), a walkway must connect the primary entrance to the ROW.
All pedestrian entrances must include an awning, canopy, recess, or some other form of shelter to provide weather protection for users.
Features used for weather protection must have a minimum depth of 4 ft for primary entrances and a minimum depth of 12 in for all other entrances.
2. Orientation of Major Service Activity
Orient major service activity areas of the development away from major streets.
Major service activity (e.g., loading, delivery, and garbage collection, etc.) must not be located on a facade that faces an adjoining major street (i.e., within 45 degrees of the street property line).
Major streets in the BI zone are: Hwy 224, International Way, Harmony Road, and 37th Avenue.
Building facades more than 200 ft from a property line along a major street may include major service activity provided there is a continuous 4-ft wide landscape buffer or another building which separates the entire length of the service activity area from the street.

3. Building Facade Design
Use color, materials, and architectural design to visually reduce the scale and impact of large buildings.
All buildings must incorporate design elements that break up all street-facing façades.
For every 60 ft of street-facing facade, a minimum of one of the following elements must be provided:
a. A recess of the building by a minimum of 2 ft deep and 6 ft long.
b. A covered walkway or recessed entry a minimum of 5 ft deep and 6 ft long.
c. A roof line offset of at least 5 ft from the top surface of one roof to the top surface of the other.
d. Extending an architectural bay at least 2 ft from the primary street-facing facade.
Exterior wall-mounted mechanical equipment is prohibited on street-facing facades.
4. Building Materials
Use building materials and features that are durable and consistent with the proposed use of the building, level of exposure to public view, and exposure to natural elements.
Prohibited materials listed as (N) in Table 19.310.6.D.1 are not permitted.
All buildings must utilize the following building materials on exterior walls of street-facing facades.
See Table 19.310.6.D.1 for the primary, secondary, and prohibited material types referenced in this standard.
a. Buildings must utilize primary materials for at least 60% of the applicable building facades.
b. Secondary materials are permitted on no greater than 40% of each applicable building facade.
c. Accent materials are permitted on no greater than 10% of each applicable building facade as trims or accents (e.g., flashing, projecting features, ornamentation, etc.)
d. Buildings must not utilize materials listed as prohibited materials (N).
e. For existing development, facade modifications that affect more than 50% of the facade must comply with standards in this subsection.
The Planning Manager may waive this requirement if application of the standards would create an incongruous appearance of existing and new materials.

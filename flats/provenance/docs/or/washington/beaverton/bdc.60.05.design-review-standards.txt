60.05.10. Design Principles.

Beaverton Development Code
Date Printed: September 28, 2026 Chapter 60, SR-2
The following design principles are general statements to guide the development of the built environment, the
appearance of that development, and the effect of that development on the existing surroundings. The design
guidelines and standards implement these principles. [ORD 4584; June 2012]
1.Building Design and Orientation. Design buildings that enhance the visual character of the community and take
into account the surrounding neighborhoods, provide permanence, and create a sense of place. In Residential,
Commercial and Multiple Use districts, design buildings that contribute to a safe, high quality pedestrian-oriented
streetscape.
2.Multiple Use District Building Orientation and Design. Locate buildings so they are conveniently and safely
accessible from on-site and off-site walkways, sidewalks and streets, and so buildings near the edge of a right of
way provide a high quality, pedestrian oriented streetscape, contribute to safety by offering "eyes on the street"
and promote pedestrian safety and use. Provide a pedestrian-friendly environment through building and site design
treatments that may vary in nature and degree depending on the character of the urban area, the characteristics of
the street, and the type of use and development proposed. [ORD 4876; January 2026]
3.Circulation and Parking Design. Provide integrated multi-modal circulation and parking improvements that are safe
and convenient, connect to surrounding neighborhoods and streets, and serve the needs of development.
4.Landscape, Open Space, and Natural Areas Design. Create landscape areas that contribute to the aesthetics of the
community, conserve, protect, enhance or restore natural features and the natural environment, provide an
attractive setting for buildings, and provide safe, interesting outdoor spaces for residents, customers, employees,
and the community. Whenever possible, utilize native vegetative species which are disease and drought tolerant.
5.Lighting Design. Provide exterior lighting for buildings, parking lots, pedestrian pathways, vehicular areas,
pedestrian plazas, public open spaces to ensure public safety and convenience, and to minimize excessive
illumination on environmentally sensitive areas, adjoining properties, and streets.
[ORD 4332, 01/01/2005; ORD 4584, 06/01/2012; ORD 4876, 01/20/2026]

60.05.11. Applicability of Design Standards to Residential Uses.

[ORD 4822; June 2022] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
For residential development, the applicability of the Design Standards and Guidelines shall vary based on the following
standards.
1. In RMA, RMB, and RMC, all allowed uses shall comply with Section 60.05.60, except Compact Detached Housing
and Multi-dwelling structures. Compact Detached Housing and Multi-dwelling structures shall comply with Sections
60.05.15 through 60.05.30 or Sections 60.05.35 through 60.05.50.
2. In CM-RM, all allowed uses shall comply with Section 60.05.60, except Compact Detached Housing and Multi-
dwelling structures.
A. Compact Detached Housing shall comply with Sections 60.05.15 through 60.05.30 or Sections 60.05.35
through 60.05.50.
B. Multi-dwellings with 5 or 6 units shall comply with Section 60.05.65.
C. In no case shall 60.05.15 through 60.05.30 or Sections 60.05.35 through 60.05.50 apply to the development
of small-scale commercial uses in the CM-RM zoning district, which shall instead comply with Section
60.05.60.
3. In all other districts, all allowed uses except single-detached dwellings (including Manufactured Homes) shall
comply with Sections 60.05.15 through 60.05.30 or Sections 60.05.35 through 60.05.50.

1.Building articulation and variety.
A. Multi-dwellings in all applicable Residential zones, and townhouses in the MR and CM-MR zones, shall be

windows, bays and offsetting walls that extend at least 18 inches, recessed entrances, doors, loading doors
and bays, and changes in material types. Different shapes, sizes, and applications of the same material are
not considered changes in material types. Changes in material types shall have a minimum dimension of two
feet and minimum area of 25 square feet. The percentage of the total square footage of elevation area

4879; April 2026]
3. 15 percent in Industrial zones. [ORD 4462; January 2008] [ORD 4859; January 2025]

building entrance or multiple tenant entrances. [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4659; July 2015]

2.Roofs and parapets.
A. All roofs with a slope of less than 4:12 pitch shall be articulated with a parapet wall that must project
vertically above the roof line at least 12 inches and incorporate either:
1. A datum line the length of the facade that is a minimum of 4 inches in depth and height or a minimum 2
inches in depth and height if it incorporates a different material from the rest of the facade, or
2. Completely recess the top one or two floors a minimum of 6 feet. [ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4859;
January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. On new buildings larger than 20,000 square feet in total floor area, roof areas with less than or equal to a
2:12 slope shall incorporate at least one of the following:
1. All the roof area, exclusive of space dedicated to mechanical systems, vents, elevator enclosures, and
skylights, shall utilize a roof with a Solar Reflectance Index of 78 or greater.
2. An Eco-Roof surface comprising a minimum of 30 percent of the total roof area or 1,000 square feet,
whichever is larger.
3. A rooftop terrace comprising a minimum of 30 percent of the total roof area or 1,000 square feet
whichever is larger. At least 30 percent of the space shall be vegetated and at least 70 percent shall be
shaded (by vegetation or structures) at 3 p.m. June 21.
4. Rooftop solar (or other renewable energy source) production of at least 0.003 kilowatt per square foot
of gross roof area. [ORD 4879; April 2026]
3.Building entrances.
A. Primary entrances
1. Non-residential and mixed-use buildings with ground-floor non-residential use(s) shall provide Primary
Entrances through one of the following methods
a. Provide a Primary Entrance for all non-residential tenant spaces; or
b. Provide a shared entrance for multiple non-residential tenant spaces; or
c. Provide a combination of primary entrances for each tenant space and shared entrances for
multiple non-residential tenant spaces.
2. Primary Entrances provided to meet 60.05.15.3.A.1 shall face the street and provide a direct pedestrian
connection to the street. Primary Entrances facing parking lots do not count toward the Primary

place a Primary Entrance door within 15 feet of the building corner. Corner lots where one street is a
freeway or freeway ramp are exempt from this standard.
3. Primary Entrances shall include weather protection that is at least 6 feet wide and 4 feet deep through
the use of arcades, roofs, canopies, porches, porticoes, recesses, structural awnings or a combination of
those methods.

c. Ground cover: Live ground cover consisting of low-height plants, shrubs, or grasses shall be planted

used but shall be limited to no more than 25 percent of the required buffer area.

hedge, fence, or wall.

60.05.35. Building Design and Orientation Guidelines.

All guidelines apply in all zoning districts unless otherwise noted in Section 60.05.11 or within the guidelines below.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
1.Building articulation and variety. [ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Residential buildings should be of a limited length in order to avoid undifferentiated building elevations,
reduce the mass of individual buildings, and create a scale of development that is pedestrian friendly and
allow circulation between buildings by pedestrians. (Standard 60.05.15.1.A)
B. Building elevations should be varied and articulated to provide visual interest to pedestrians. Within larger
projects, variations in architectural elements such as: building elevations, roof levels, architectural features,
and exterior finishes should be provided. (Standards 60.05.15.1.A and B)
C. To balance horizontal features on longer building elevations, vertical building elements, such as building
entries, should be emphasized. (Standard 60.05.15.1.B)
D. Buildings should promote and enhance a comfortable pedestrian scale and orientation. This guideline does
not apply to buildings in Industrial districts where the principal use of the building is manufacturing,
assembly, fabricating, processing, packing, storage, wholesale or distribution activities. (Standard
60.05.15.1.B) [ORD 4531; April 2010]
E. Building elevations entirely or partially visible from and within 200 feet of an adjacent street or major parking
area should be articulated with architectural features such as windows, dormers, off-setting walls, alcoves,
balconies or bays, or by other design features that reflect the building's structural system. Undifferentiated
blank walls facing a street, common green, shared court or major parking area should be avoided. (Standards
60.05.15.1.B, C and D) [ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4857; October 2024]
F. Building elevations entirely or partially visible from and within 100 feet of an adjacent street where the
principal use of the building is manufacturing, assembly, fabricating, processing, packing, storage and
wholesale and distribution activities in an Industrial zoning district, should be articulated with architectural
features such as windows, dormers, off-setting walls, alcoves, balconies or bays, or by other design features
that reflect the building's structural system. Undifferentiated blank walls facing a street should be avoided.
(Standards 60.05.15.1.B and C) [ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
2.Roofs and parapets. [ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
Beaverton Development Code
Date Printed: September 28, 2026 Chapter 60, SR-43
A. Flat or low slope roofs should include a roofline that provides visual interest and establish a such as cornice
treatments. (Standard 60.05.15.2.A) [ORD 4782; April 2020] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Roofs on new buildings larger than 20,000 square feet in total floor area shall include sustainability features
while allowing other rooftop uses essential to the building function and tenant needs. (Standard
60.05.15.2.B) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
3.Building entrances.
A. All buildings should be oriented toward and accessed from the street. Entries on streets should be more
prominent than those facing parking lots. Primary entrances should provide weather protection and should
incorporate changes in mass, surface, or finish to emphasize the entrance. On corner lots where neither
street is a local street, the design of buildings at the intersection of two such streets shall provide an entry at
or near the corner unless site constraints, physical constraints associated with the building, or safety
concerns make a corner entry infeasible. (Standard 60.05.15.3.A) [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4879; April
2026]
B. Special attention should be given to designing primary building entrances that are both attractive and
functional. Primary entrances should provide weather protection and should incorporate changes in mass,
surface, or finish to emphasize the entrance. Shared Entrances should be made visually prominent and
receive architectural emphasis. (Standard 60.05.15.3.B) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
4.Exterior building materials.
A. Exterior building materials and finishes should convey an impression of permanence and durability. Materials

60.05.15. Building Design and Orientation Standards.

All standards apply in all zoning districts unless otherwise noted in Section 60.05.11 or within the standards below.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
1.Building articulation and variety.
A. Multi-dwellings in all applicable Residential zones, and townhouses in the MR and CM-MR zones, shall be limited in length to 200 feet.
[ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4822; June 2022] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
B. Buildings that have any portion of any elevation visible from and within 200 feet of an adjacent public street shall have a minimum portion of the street-facing elevation(s) and the elevation(s) containing a primary building entrance or multiple tenant entrances devoted to Permanent Architectural Features designed to provide articulation and variety.
These permanent features include, but are not limited to, windows, bays and offsetting walls that extend at least 18 inches, recessed entrances, doors, loading doors and bays, and changes in material types.
Different shapes, sizes, and applications of the same material are not considered changes in material types.
Changes in material types shall have a minimum dimension of two feet and minimum area of 25 square feet.
The percentage of the total square footage of elevation area is: [ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
1. 30 percent in applicable Residential zones, and all uses in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
[ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
2. 50 percent in Commercial zones where glazing is less than 35 percent.
[ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
3. 15 percent in Industrial zones.
[ORD 4462; January 2008] [ORD 4859; January 2025] In Industrial zones, where the principal use of the building is manufacturing, assembly, fabricating, processing, packing, storage, wholesale or distribution activities, the above standards shall apply only to elevations visible from and within 100 feet of an adjacent public street, and elevations that include a primary building entrance or multiple tenant entrances.
[ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4659; July 2015]
C. The maximum spacing between Permanent Architectural Features (to meet the requirements of Section 60.05.15.1.B), both vertically and horizontally, shall be no more than: [ORD 4782; April 2020] [ORD 4857;
October 2024]
1. 40 feet in applicable Residential zones, and all uses in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
[ORD 4584;
June 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
2. 60 feet in Industrial zones.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
3. 15 feet in detached residential developments in Multiple Use zones for walls facing streets, common greens, and shared courts.
[ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
D. In addition to the requirements of Section 60.05.15.1.B and C., detached and attached residential building elevations facing a street, common green or shared court shall not contain an undifferentiated wall area greater than 150 square feet within which a box with the minimum dimensions of 10 feet by 15 feet can fit.
For the purposes of this standard, undifferentiated means lacking Permanent Architectural Features.
[ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4857; October 2024]
2.Roofs and parapets.
A. All roofs with a slope of less than 4:12 pitch shall be articulated with a parapet wall that must project vertically above the roof line at least 12 inches and incorporate either:
1. A datum line the length of the facade that is a minimum of 4 inches in depth and height or a minimum 2 inches in depth and height if it incorporates a different material from the rest of the facade, or
2. Completely recess the top one or two floors a minimum of 6 feet.
[ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4859;
January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. On new buildings larger than 20,000 square feet in total floor area, roof areas with less than or equal to a 2:12 slope shall incorporate at least one of the following:
1. All the roof area, exclusive of space dedicated to mechanical systems, vents, elevator enclosures, and skylights, shall utilize a roof with a Solar Reflectance Index of 78 or greater.
2. An Eco-Roof surface comprising a minimum of 30 percent of the total roof area or 1,000 square feet, whichever is larger.
3. A rooftop terrace comprising a minimum of 30 percent of the total roof area or 1,000 square feet whichever is larger.
At least 30 percent of the space shall be vegetated and at least 70 percent shall be shaded (by vegetation or structures) at 3 p.m.
June 21.
4. Rooftop solar (or other renewable energy source) production of at least 0.003 kilowatt per square foot of gross roof area.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
3.Building entrances.
A. Primary entrances
1. Non-residential and mixed-use buildings with ground-floor non-residential use(s) shall provide Primary Entrances through one of the following methods
a. Provide a Primary Entrance for all non-residential tenant spaces; or
b. Provide a shared entrance for multiple non-residential tenant spaces; or
c. Provide a combination of primary entrances for each tenant space and shared entrances for multiple non-residential tenant spaces.
2. Primary Entrances provided to meet 60.05.15.3.A.1 shall face the street and provide a direct pedestrian connection to the street.
Primary Entrances facing parking lots do not count toward the Primary Entrance requirements of this section.
Buildings on corner lots where neither street is a local street shall place a Primary Entrance door within 15 feet of the building corner.
Corner lots where one street is a freeway or freeway ramp are exempt from this standard.
3. Primary Entrances shall include weather protection that is at least 6 feet wide and 4 feet deep through the use of arcades, roofs, canopies, porches, porticoes, recesses, structural awnings or a combination of those methods.
B. Shared entrances provided to meet 60.05.15.A.1 shall include one of the following features in the entry design to highlight the entry:
1. Corner entries within 8 feet of the longest street-facing façade and at an angle of up to 45 degrees from said street;
2. An entry recessed at least 36 inches from the surrounding wall plane that has a width of at least 8 feet;
3. Projecting entry a minimum of 8 feet wide and 36 inches from the surrounding wall plane; or
4. Entry courtyard a minimum of 40 square feet.
The courtyard shall provide two of the following site elements:
a. 20 square feet of landscaping, in ground or in planters.
b. Pedestrian-scale lighting.
c. Benches or seating that provide at least 10 linear feet of seating.
d. A water feature.
e. Other element as approved by the decision-making authority.
[ORD 4879; April 2026] 4.Exterior building materials.
A. For Permitted non-residential uses and Conditional Uses in applicable Residential zones and all uses in Commercial and Multiple Use zones (except detached residential uses fronting streets, common greens and shared courts), a maximum of 30 percent of each elevation that is entirely or partially visible from and within 200 feet of a public street or a public park, public plaza or other public open space, and on elevations that include a primary building entrance or multiple tenant entrances, may be plain, smooth, unfinished concrete, concrete block, plywood and sheet pressboard.
The remaining elevation area for all applicable uses in all applicable zones shall be architecturally treated.
Appropriate methods of architectural treatment shall include, but are not limited to, scoring, changes in material texture, and the application of other finish materials such as wood, rock, brick or tile wall treatment.
[ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
This standard shall also apply to all uses in the Industrial zones, except for buildings containing manufacturing, fabricating, processing, packing, storage and wholesale and distribution facilities as a principal use of the site where this standard shall apply only to the primary elevation that is entirely or partially visible from and within 200 feet of a public street or a public park, public plaza or other public open space.
[ORD 4857; October 2024]
[ORD 4531; April 2010]
B. For Permitted non-residential uses and Conditional Uses in applicable Residential zones and all uses in Commercial and Multiple Use zones, plain, smooth, exposed concrete and concrete block used as foundation material shall not be more than 3 feet above the finished grade level adjacent to the foundation wall, unless pigmented, textured, or both.
In Industrial districts, foundations may extend up to 4 feet above the finished grade level.
[ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
5.Roof-mounted equipment.
A. All roof-mounted equipment shall be screened from view from adjacent streets or adjacent properties in one of the following ways:
1. A parapet wall; or
2. A screen around the equipment that is made of a primary exterior finish material used on other portions of the building; or
3. Setback from the street-facing elevation such that it is not visible from the public street(s); or
4. Screened from view by another building.
[ORD 4782; April 2020]
B. As shown in the diagram below, the vertical measuring distance for required screening shall be measured at 5 feet above the finished or existing grade at the property line or public right-of-way abutting the development site's front yard setback for a distance of 100 lineal feet measured outward from the development site's front property line.
Once the vertical measuring distance is established for the site's front yard, this same vertical measuring distance shall be applied to all sides of the development site's perimeter property lines.
[ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4782; April 2020] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
C. Solar panels, dishes/antennas, pipes, vents, and chimneys are exempt from this standard. 6.Building location and orientation along streets in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
[ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Excepting buildings in the CM-CS zone and buildings used for non-residential or multiple uses in the CM-HDR zone, buildings shall occupy a minimum percentage of a site frontage between the minimum setback and the maximum setback established in the zoning district’s site development standards or 16 feet if there is no maximum setback listed in the site development standards.
Accessory structures, non-habitable buildings, and commercial storage structures that do not require building permits shall not be used to satisfy this standard.
1. At least 60 percent of the site’s Primary Frontage on streets or Primary Accessways shall be occupied by a building or buildings.
The Primary Frontage shall be determined in order of importance as follows:
a. Street with Frequent Transit Service
b. Street with non-frequent transit service
c. Street with the highest classification, (excepting those with freeway or principal arterial functional classifications)
d. A Primary Accessway
2. 35 percent of the abutting secondary public street frontage where a parcel abuts more than one public street or Primary Accessway.
When a parcel abuts more than two public streets or Primary Accessway, the applicant shall designate the secondary frontage at time of initial development, and there shall be no frontage requirement on any remaining abutting public streets or Primary Accessways.
3. For multi-dwelling buildings with a central courtyard space that opens to a street or Primary Accessway, at least 75 percent of the site’s frontage on any street or Primary Accessway shall be occupied by buildings and the central courtyard.
4. For the purpose of calculating the percentage of site frontage occupied by a building the following maybe subtracted from the site frontage length:
a. The width of the widest part of the driveway’s public element for a maximum of one driveway; and
b. Areas determined to be unbuildable due to sight clearance and sight distance requirements in the Engineering Design Manual; and
c. Plazas up to 20 percent of the total public street frontage per site.
To qualify, the plaza shall be placed next to or in front of a building that is set back no more than 40 feet from the right of way.

When located between a building and a sidewalk, direct pedestrian access between the building and sidewalk must be provided to the abutting building; and
d. Natural Areas.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. All buildings in the CM-CS zone and buildings used for non-residential or multiple uses in the CM-HDR zone shall occupy a minimum public street frontage as follows:
1. 50 percent of the abutting Primary Frontage.
When a parcel abuts more than one public street, the primary public street frontage shall be determined in the following descending order:
a. Property line abutting a designated Neighborhood Route street or the property line where any portion is across the street from a Public Park;
b. Property line abutting a designated Collector street; or
c. In the absence of a public street frontage meeting a. or b. above, the applicant shall designate the primary public street frontage at the time of initial development.
2. 35 percent of the abutting secondary public street frontage where a parcel abuts more than one public street.
When a parcel abuts more than two public streets, the applicant shall designate the secondary public street frontage at time of initial development, and there shall be no public street frontage requirement on any remaining abutting public streets.
3. The following shall be subtracted from the calculations of total primary and secondary public street frontages:
a. The width of the widest part of a driveway’s public element;
b. Areas determined to be unbuildable due to sight clearance and sight distance requirements in the Engineering Design Manual;
c. Existing Public Utility Easements;
d. Plazas, including Pedestrian Plazas, up to 20 percent of the total public street frontage per site;
e. Natural Areas; and
f. The width of the travel surface of trails, up to a maximum of 20 feet.
This exemption does not apply when any portion of a trail that travels parallel to any portion of a property line abutting a street.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
C. Vehicle parking and circulation areas within 10 feet of a street lot line shall be limited to no more than 35 percent of the length of any street lot line.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Areas within 10 feet of any street lot line that are not occupied by a building or vehicle area shall be landscaped to the standards of Section 60.05.25 or hardscaped for pedestrian use.
[ORD 4879; April 2026] 7.Height Transition.
A. On the portion of the site less than or equal to 30 feet from the lot line of a lot zoned RMA, RMB, RMC, or a comparable Washington County zone as indicated in Table 1.5.2 of the Comprehensive Plan, the maximum building height shall be 60 feet or the maximum height permitted in the abutting residential district, whichever is greater.
This standard does not apply when the property zoned RMA, RMB, or RMC, or a comparable Washington County zone is not vacant and contains no dwellings.
[ORD 4879; April 2026] 8.Ground floor elevations in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
A. Ground-floor elevations entirely or partially visible from and within 200 feet of a public street, Primary Accessway, public park, public plaza or other public open space, and elevations that include a Primary Entrance or multiple tenant entrances, shall have the following minimum percentage of the ground-floor elevation area between 2 and 10 feet in height from finish grade permanently treated with windows or
glass doorway openings.
All glass used to meet these requirements shall have a Visible Transmittance (VT) value of 0.6 or greater.
[ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
1. Non-residential uses: 50 percent for Primary Frontages and 40 percent for non-primary frontages [ORD 4879; April 2026]
2. Multi-Dwelling residential uses: 30 percent [ORD 4879; April 2026]
3. Townhouse residential uses 25 percent [ORD 4879; April 2026]
4. Primary public street frontages in CM-CS and CM-HDR: 50 percent.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
5. Secondary public street frontages in CM-CS and CM-HDR zoning districts: 35 percent.
[ORD 4859;
January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Structured parking shall have a minimum of 30 percent wall openings that utilize decorative screening systems.
All openings shall be no less than 3.5 feet from the top of the parking slab so vehicle headlights do not trespass beyond the building facade.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. Buildings not used exclusively for residential use shall provide weather protection such as permanent awnings, canopies, overhangs, or architectural features.
Such weather protection shall meet all the following standards:
1. Weather protection shall be provided:
a. For a minimum 50 percent of the length of Primary Frontage elevations and 35 percent on other street-facing elevations, or
b. Over all ground-floor entries and windows.
2. Weather protection shall have a minimum depth of 4 feet that may be achieved through projections or recesses from the primary facade, or a combination thereof.
3. Weather protection shall be provided over the public right of way or hardscaped area within a pedestrian amenity space with a clear area at least 3 feet in depth between the building and the street.
4. Weather protection that extends over the public right of way shall comply with the Oregon Structural Specialty Code in effect at the time of application for projections or encroachments into the public right-of-way.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Buildings taller than 30 feet shall have a defined base on building elevations visible from and within 200 feet of a public street and on elevations that include a primary building entrance or multiple tenant entrances by using one of the following methods: [ORD 4879; April 2026]
1. Floor-to-floor height of the ground floor is a minimum of 3 feet taller than the average of the remaining floor-to-floor heights.
2. Ground-floor level is set back a minimum of 2 feet from the primary building wall for at least 70 percent of the building elevation length.
3. All floors above the ground-floor level are set back a minimum of 2 feet from the ground-floor level for at least 70 percent of the building elevation length.
4. A datum line is provided between the ground floor and second floor.
The datum line shall be a minimum of 4 inches in depth and height.
The datum line may be a minimum of 2 inches in depth and height if the primary exterior building material, excluding windows, changes between the first and second floor.
The datum line may project or be recessed.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
9.Compact Detached Housing design.
[ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Primary building entrances and porches accessing a primary entrance shall face streets, common greens or shared courts and must meet the requirements of Section 60.05.15.3.
Porches, if provided, shall have clear dimensions of at least 6 feet wide and 4 feet deep, and shall be covered by a roof.
The primary entrance must
be within 4 feet of grade, as measured as the average grade along the foundation of the longest wall of the building elevation containing the primary entrance or porch.
[ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
B. Elevations on residential units facing streets, common greens and shared courts shall have a minimum of 25 percent of the elevation area on each floor permanently treated with windows.
C. Windows shall be vertical or square in proportion.
Horizontal windows may be created when vertical windows or a mixture of vertical and other shaped windows are grouped together, or there is a row of clerestory windows across the top of the grouped windows.
D. [ORD 4576; January 2012] Alleys (including access easements) and shared courts are the preferred options to serve garages, and shall be provided on all lots except where topography, or other identified physical constraints preclude their use.
Within a project, the majority of units shall have garages accessed from alleys or shared courts.
E. When parking is provided in a garage facing a street or shared court, the following standards must be met:
1. No more than 50 percent of the horizontal length of the ground floor elevation facing a street or shared court shall be an attached garage door entrance (i.e., garage doorway) or 12 feet long, whichever is greater.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
2. Garages shall be recessed at least 1.5 feet from the ground floor front of the dwelling.
[ORD 4859;
January 2025]
3. There may be no more than two individual garage doors per dwelling unit.
4. The width of the driveway (at the back of the sidewalk) shall not exceed 12 feet for single lane and 16 feet for double lane driveways.
[ORD 4576; January 2012]
5. A maximum of 50 percent of the units within a project shall have driveways 16 feet in width.
No two lots of this configuration shall be adjacent to one another.
[ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025]
F. Garages that face a street or shared court shall contain one or more of the following design features.
Garages shall not terminate the view into shared courts from a public street unless they contain two or more of the following design features:
1. Garage trellis or pergola extending at least 12 inches from the building face.
2. Windows on 15 percent of the garage door.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
3. Decorative hardware.
4. Natural wood finish.
5. A recess of at least 3 feet.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
6. Multiple materials finish or colors are used.
G. Residential structures must have a roof that meets or exceeds the requirements of Section 60.05.15.2.
H. There must be architectural detailing that varies from unit to unit.
Architectural detailing includes but is not limited to the following list.
Some design features include requirements that exceed a minimum standard found elsewhere in this code.
Each dwelling shall utilize at least 5 of the following design features: [ORD 4859; January 2025]
1. The use of different exterior siding materials.
In general, materials should change on horizontal planes, not vertical planes.
Types of siding materials include:
a. Horizontal lap siding, including simulated horizontal lap siding where the boards in the pattern are 6 inches or less in width;
b. Vertical cedar siding;
c. Beveled siding,
d. Shingles
e. Stucco
f. Brick
g. Stone
h. Scored masonry
i. Changes in a combination of texture, pattern or color of a single material
2. A primary sloped roof that is no flatter than 6/12 and no steeper than 12/12.
(Exceeds the requirements of Section 60.05.15.2.).
3. A tile or shake roof.
4. One or more dormers on the front elevation.
5. Three or more gables.
6. Elevations on residential units facing streets, common greens and shared courts having at least 40 percent of the elevation area on each floor permanently treated with windows (Exceeds the requirements of Section 60.05.15.9.B.).
7. Window shutters on windows which face streets, common greens or shared courts.
8. Bay or bowed windows on the front elevation.
9. Trim marking roof lines, porches, windows and doors on all elevations.
The trim must be at least 3.5 inches wide.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
10. Weather protection for primary building entrances that exceed the minimum area requirements of
Section 60.05.15.3 by 25 percent.

11. Porches on the front elevation that have clear dimensions of at least eight feet wide and six feet deep, and are covered by a roof supported by structurally integral columns or brackets.
12. Balcony on the same façade as the main entrance.
The balcony must be at least 48 square feet and a minimum 8 feet wide and must be accessible from the interior living space of the house.
13. An attached garage with a gable or hip roof, or with a second story above the garage.
14. Building face or roof offsets (minimum 12 inch offset) on the front elevation.
15. Permanent planter boxes of at least 25 square feet constructed as an extension of a front elevation or primary building entrance.
16. A landscaped courtyard of at least 100 square feet located as a transition element between a primary building entrance and a street, common green or shared court.
The landscaping within the courtyard shall exceed the minimum requirements of Section 60.05.25.4 by 15 percent.

17. Other architectural or design elements that apply to at least 10 percent of the front elevation and result in visual interest, variety and beauty.
I. Building elevations shall not be repeated across a street, shared court or common green from each other or on adjacent parcels.
In these instances, elevations shall have at least 5 different design details as described in
Section H. above.

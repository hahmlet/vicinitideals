60.05.45. Landscape, Open Space and Natural Areas Design Guidelines.

All guidelines apply in all zoning districts unless otherwise noted in Section 60.05.11 or within the guidelines below.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
1.Landscape requirements for residential developments consisting of 2 or 3 units of Attached Housing or Compact Detached Housing.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. Sites shall be landscaped with live plantings in areas where planting is possible to prevent stormwater runoff to avoid negative water quality effects and prevent environmental damage of water quantity runoff; reduce heat island effects; provide opportunities for shade; and contribute to incorporating natural features in development for ecosystem benefits as well as mental health benefits for people.
(Section 60.05.25.1.) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
2.Minimum Open Space and landscape requirements for residential developments consisting of 4 to 7 units of Attached Housing or Compact Detached Housing.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. For Attached Dwellings, sufficient landscaping with live planting and Usable Open Space elements shall be provided where possible to provide opportunities for people to gather; prevent stormwater runoff to avoid negative water quality effects and prevent environmental damage of water quantity runoff; reduce heat island effects; provide opportunities for shade; and contribute to incorporating natural features in development for ecosystem benefits as well as mental health benefits for people.
(Section 60.05.25.2.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. For Compact Detached Housing, sufficient private patio or yard area shall be provided to ensure the space is usable and promotes access to the outdoors and allows people to gather.
(Section 60.05.25.2.B) [ORD 4879;
April 2026]
3.Minimum Usable Open Space and landscape requirements for residential developments consisting of 8 or more units of Attached Housing or Compact Detached Housing; non-residential developments; and Mixed Use developments.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. Open Space requirements
1. Non-residential buildings, including Mixed Use buildings with no residential uses, shall provide tenants and/or visitors access to high-quality Usable Open Spaces in a sufficient amount so people have places to gather.
They may include a combination of Publicly Accessible Open Spaces and Shared Open Spaces.(Standard 60.05.25.3.A)
2. Residential-only buildings shall provide residents with access to high-quality Usable Open Spaces that provide areas to gather and may include a combination of Publicly Accessible Open Spaces, Shared Open Spaces, Private Open Spaces, Common Community Rooms and, if part of a Compact Detached Housing development, Compact Detached Housing Common Greens and Compact Detached Housing Shared Courts.
(Standard 60.05.25.3.A)
3. Mixed Use buildings with residential uses shall provide residents with access to high-quality Usable Open Spaces that provide areas to gather and may include a combination of Publicly Accessible Open Spaces, Shared Open Spaces, Common Community Rooms, and Private Open Spaces.
(Standard 60.05.25.3.A)
4. For 60.05.25.3.A.1 through 5, ratios and percentages for Publicly Accessible Open Spaces, Compact Detached Housing Common Greens, Compact Detached Housing Shared Courts and Common Community Rooms may be modified by the decision-making authority if the modifications provide access to high-quality, usable open spaces that provide areas to gather for consistent with the development proposed considering the current and potential future users of the development.
5. For 60.05.25.3.A.6 and 7, dimensions may be adjusted by the decision-making authority if the open space features remain a reasonable distance from the people they are supposed to serve and the routes
of travel between the buildings and the open space features are accessible to people with disabilities and are safe and comfortable to navigate by people walking, biking, and rolling.
6. For 60.05.25.3.A.8, exterior Usable Open Space features near Collector and higher functional classification streets shall design the space to prevent motor vehicles from entering the space in a manner that could injure people using the space.
7. For 60.05.25.3.A.9, vehicular circulation areas and parking areas shall not count toward Usable Open Space unless they meet the specifications of Usable Open Space, the areas are designed as shared streets consistent with city-adopted standards such as those in the Engineering Design Manual, and the primary use for the space is for pedestrians and automobile use is at slow speeds and very limited in volume.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Site landscaping shall be provided on sites as follows:
1. Sites shall be landscaped with live plants to provide some of the following benefits: prevent stormwater runoff; avoid negative water quality effects and prevent environmental damage of water quantity runoff; reduce heat island effects; provide opportunities for shade; contribute to incorporating natural features in development for ecosystem benefits as well as mental health benefits for people.
The landscaping requirement may be reduced by the decision-making authority from the Section 60.05.25 requirement if the applicant demonstrates that there are unusual site constraints that make the amount infeasible while achieving the above benefits to the maximum extent feasible.
(Section 60.05.25.3.B.1)
2. The decision-making authority may adjust the percentage of landscaping required by Section 60.05.25.3.A that can count toward the requirement in Section 60.05.25.3.C.1 if it is determined that the proposal still achieves the purpose of the Usable Open Space and landscaping requirements and achieves some of the outcomes in B.1 above.
(Section 60.05.25. 3.B.2)
3. Paved areas with no live plantings shall not be considered required landscaped area.
(Section 60.05.25.3.B.3)
4. Landscaping shall include diverse tree and shrub plantings to provide visual interest across the site, including in color, seasonal foliage, and scale, and shall be planted at a density that provides sufficient opportunities for shade in areas not occupied by structures, paving, or hardscaped areas that are of a sufficient size and have sufficient soil density to support trees.
(Section 60.05.25. 3.B.4)
5. Landscaped areas shall be designed to create sustainable, attractive developments that are consistent with the uses on site, prevent erosion, and preserve and enhance nature.
Mulch shall be used sparingly and shall have a material and color that is appropriate for the uses on site and contributes to site aesthetics.
(Section 60.05.25. 3.B.5) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
4.General landscaping. [ORD 4859; January 2025]
A. New plantings shall be selected from approved plant lists.
(Section 60.05.25.4.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Irrigation should be provided as appropriate, based on plant species and site conditions, to ensure proper establishment of plantings in all landscaped areas.
(Standard 60.05.25.6.A)
C. Landscape plantings shall be specified and planted consistent with the standards.
Variations may be allowed if the proposed substitutions achieve the same effect with the same or better expected survivability and relevant contribution to the ecological, stormwater management, and aesthetic outcomes of the landscape plan.
(Standard 60.05.25.4.C) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Landscaping shall include diverse tree and shrub plantings to provide visual interest across the site, including in color, seasonal foliage, and scale.
The use of one tree species for over half of new tree plantings is discouraged.
(Standard 60.05.25.4.D) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
E. Use of native and drought-tolerant trees and vegetation shall be incorporated for compatibility with local and regional climatic conditions and to reduce water use devoted to landscaping.
(Standards 60.05.25.4.E and F) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
5.Usable Open Space.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. Publicly Accessible Open Spaces (PAOS) shall be designed to create Usable Open Space for public use.
PAOS may include pedestrian paths, pedestrian refuge areas, landscaped gardens, places to rest and relax, places to play, and places to gather and socialize.
The PAOS must be a sufficient size and incorporate sufficient elements to accommodate public use and make it an attractive place to rest, relax, or socialize.
(Standard 60.05.25.5.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Shared Open Spaces shall be at least partially open to the sky and be designed to be usable for tenants and guests for a variety of communal activities and uses.
Shared Open Spaces may include pedestrian paths, landscaped gardens, places to rest and relax, places to play, and places to gather and socialize.
(Standard 60.05.25.5.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. Common Community Rooms shall be easily accessible by building occupants and designed to serve as gathering places and accessory spaces to Shared Open Spaces or PAOS.
Common Community Rooms may include lounges, fitness rooms, shared kitchens, dining areas, co-working spaces, game rooms, or other spaces that provide opportunities for shared experiences.
(Standard 60.05.25.5.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Private Open Spaces shall be designed to create usable outdoor space for residents to spend time outdoors.
E. Usable Open Spaces shall be close enough to the building or buildings where residents live so the residents can easily access them by walking or using a mobility device.
Usable Open Space for non-residential buildings or the non-residential portions of Mixed Use buildings shall be within a reasonable distance by walking or using a mobility device so the expected tenants or visitors will be able to locate and take advantage of the Usable OpenSpace.
(Standard 60.05.25.5.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
F. Usable Open Spaces close to high-traffic streets shall include design elements that prevent crashes from injuring people within the Usable Open Space.
(Standard 60.05.25.3.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026] 6.Common Greens.
[ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Common greens should be designed to provide access for only pedestrians and bicycles to abutting properties.
Common greens should also serve as a common open space amenity for residents.
(Standard 60.05.25.6 and 60.05.25.6.A.1) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. The size of the common green right-of-way should be sufficient to accommodate expected users and uses.
The size must take into consideration the characteristics of the site and vicinity, such as the pedestrian system, structures, natural features, and the community activities that may occur within the common green.
(Standard 60.05.25.6.A.2) [ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. When a public pedestrian connection is proposed it should be designed as a distinct feature to distinguish it from an adjacent common green.
(Standards 60.05.25.6.A.3 and 4) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879;
April 2026]
D. Common greens should not provide access to parking.
(Standard 60.05.25.6.A.5) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
[ORD 4542; June 2010]
7.Shared Courts. [ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Shared courts should safely accommodate pedestrians and vehicles within the same circulation area and provide safe access to abutting properties.
Special paving and other street elements should be designed to encourage slow vehicle speeds and to signify the shared court's intended use by pedestrians as well as vehicles.
(Standards 60.05.25.8.A and B) [ORD 4859; January 2025]
B. The size and length of a shared court should be sufficient to accommodate expected users and uses.
The size and length should take into consideration the characteristics of the site and vicinity, such as the pedestrian system, structures, traffic safety, natural features, and the community activities that may occur within the shared court.
(Standards 60.05.25.8.A and B) [ORD 4859; January 2025]

8.Retaining walls. Retaining walls over 6 feet in height or greater than 50 feet in length should be architecturally treated, incorporated into the overall landscape plan, or screened by landscape material.
(Standard 60.05.25.8) [ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
9.Fences and walls.
A. Fences and walls should be constructed of attractive, durable materials.
(Standard 60.05.25.9) [ORD 4576;
January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Fences and walls constructed in front yards adjacent to public streets should provide the opportunity to view into the setback from the street unless high traffic volumes or other conflicts warrant greater security and protection.
(Standard 60.05.25.9.E) [ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
10.Changes to existing on-site surface contours at residential property lines.
The perimeters of properties should be graded in a manner to avoid conflicts with abutting residential properties such as drainage impacts, damage to tree root zones, and blocking sunlight.
(Standard 60.05.25.10) [ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
11.Integrate water quality, quantity, or both facilities.
Above-ground stormwater detention and treatment facilities should be integrated into the design of a development site and, if visible from a public street, should appear as a component of the landscape design.
(Standard 60.05.25.11) [ORD 4576; January 2012] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
12.Natural areas. Natural features that are indigenous to a development site, such as streams, wetlands, and mature trees should be preserved, enhanced and integrated when reasonably possible into the development plan.

13.Landscape buffering. [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. All landscape buffers shall be planted with a variety of trees, shrubs, and groundcover that will provide attractive and effective screening year-round.
Plantings should be installed within the buffer in a way that maximizes screening effectiveness.
Spacing trees more than 50 linear feet apart is discouraged.
(Standard 60.05.25.13) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. When the anticipated impacts and scale of adjacent uses are similar and conflicts between uses are not expected, less dense plantings and a narrower buffer width may be appropriate.
If buffer width or plantings are reduced, evergreen trees and hedging shrubs should be prioritized and should be arranged in a way that provides effective screening between uses.
(Standard 60.05.25.13) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. Non-residential development shall install a dense landscape buffer along property lines that abut residential uses, including vacant lots in residential zoning districts, in a way that provides landscape screening and, if necessary to mitigate anticipated visual or noise impacts, an attractive and durable structural barrier between properties.
Buffer widths less than 10 feet are discouraged, except when unique site constraints necessitate a reduction in one or more areas along a property line or when the anticipated impacts of the development are minimal.
(Standard 60.05.25.13) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. When potential noise impacts of a Conditional Use are determined to be significant enough to require noise attenuation, the development shall provide a dense landscape buffer with a structural barrier that is designed to provide complete visual screening and sufficient noise attenuation.
If a development does not provide a solid, continuous masonry wall to mitigate noise impacts, the applicant should submit a study by a licensed acoustical engineer demonstrating that the proposed structural barrier or other aspects of the site or building design will provide sufficient noise attenuation to address impacts.
Buffer widths less than 10 feet are strongly discouraged adjacent to residential uses and vacant residentially zoned property.
(Standards 60.05.25.13) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
E. A landscape buffer with a minimum width of 25 feet shall be provided along side and rear property lines that abut the Cooper Mountain Nature Park.
The buffer shall be densely planted with native varieties of trees, shrubs, and groundcover in a way that creates an attractive and natural-looking transition to the existing vegetation along the boundary of the Cooper Mountain Nature Park.
Evergreen shrubs and trees are preferred.
(Standards 60.05.25.13) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
14.Community Gardens. [ORD 4859; January 2025]
A. Community Gardens should be fenced using durable material(s) and should be designed primarily for safety and access of users and protection of garden plots.
(Standard 60.05.25.14.A) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Community Gardens should be appropriately sized and organized to provide useable and easily accessible garden plots.
Community Gardens over 1 acre in size are discouraged.
(Standard 60.05.25.14.B) [ORD 4879;
April 2026]

B. Public Plaza design.
Required public plazas should be designed, located, and furnished to provide an attractive, safe, and highly visible space for users of all ages and abilities to rest, gather, and interact with others.
Public plazas should be predominantly open to the sky and should include generous amounts of seating and landscaping to create a comfortable and inviting environment.
Wherever feasible, primary building entrances should be accessed from the public plaza.
Ample glazing should be used along abutting ground-floor building elevations when appropriate to allow views into buildings from the plaza.
(Standard 60.05.25.16.B.2) [ORD 4879; April 2026]

For the purposes of this sub-section, “site” shall mean the portion of the site affected by the application, including any area affected by grading; development of buildings and structures; pavement removal or installation; or open space or landscaping removal or installation.

d. One shrub for every 20 square feet of required landscape area.
e. Preserved trees or shrubs shall meet the standards in Section 60.05.25.4 to count toward the landscaping requirements in this section.
f. Live ground cover shall be provided in the portion of the required landscape area not covered by trees or shrubs.
Bare gravel, rock, bark or other similar materials may be used but shall not be a substitute for ground cover plantings and shall be limited to no more than 25 percent of the required landscape area.
5. All site areas not planted with trees, shrubs or other vegetated landscaping and also not occupied by structures, hardscaped areas (including paved areas), and sensitive natural areas shall be planted with live ground cover plants or other plants as well as turf grasses.
Mulch, as a ground cover, shall be composed of a naturally occurring material, have a natural color, and confined to areas underneath plants and within areas expected to be underneath plants at maturity.
Mulch is not a substitute for ground cover plants.

4.General Landscaping Requirements.
The following standards apply to new landscaping required by Section 60.05.25:
A. New plantings shall be selected from the following approved plant lists:
1. City of Beaverton Tree List
2. Clean Water Services’ Design and Construction Standards, Appendix A (2019)
3. Metro Native Plants Booklet (2018)
4. Portland Plant List (2016)
B. Irrigation shall be provided to ensure all site plantings will survive their establishment period.
Establishment period irrigation shall be provided through one of the following options or a combination of options:
1. A permanent, in-ground irrigation system with an automatic controller.
2. An irrigation system designed and certified by a licensed landscape architect as part of a landscape plan that provides sufficient water to ensure that the plants will become established.
The system does not have to be permanent if a licensed landscape architect certifies that the plants chosen can be adequately served by the proposed irrigation system.
3. Irrigation by hand for a maximum of 500 square feet per site.
C. Landscaping shall be planted at sizes no less than the following (measures shall be taken based on the American Standard for Nursery Stock ANSI standards).
In the case of a code conflict, the higher requirement shall be met.
1. Deciduous canopy trees shall be a minimum of  1.5-inch caliper size, balled and burlapped; and
2. Deciduous ornamental trees shall be a minimum of 1.5-inch caliper size, balled and burlapped; and
3. Evergreen trees shall be a minimum of 8 feet in height, balled and burlapped; and
4. Evergreen and deciduous shrubs shall be a minimum of 24 inches high from finished grade and a minimum of 1 gallon in size, except dwarf shrubs such as boxwood, which have no minimum size; and
5. Ferns and perennials shall be at least 1 gallon in size; and
6. Ground-covers plants including ornamental grasses shall be at least 4-inch pot size.
7. Areas subject to Clean Water Services regulations including stormwater facilities, vegetated corridors, and sensitive natural areas shall be planted consistent with Clean Water Services requirements.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Plant diversity:
1. If more than 17 trees are provided on a site, no more than 33 percent of the trees can be of one species; and
2. If more than 25 shrubs are provided on a site, no more than 75 percent can be of one species.
E. A minimum of 25 percent of landscape plantings provided on a site shall be drought-tolerant species as identified on an approved plant list.
F. A minimum of 50 percent of trees and 50 percent of shrubs provided on a site shall be native species, as identified in one of the plant lists in Section 60.05.25.4.A.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]

7.Shared Courts for Compact Detached Housing.
Shared courts are intended to serve pedestrians and vehicles within the same circulation area, while ensuring that all can use the area safely.
See Figure 3.
Special paving and other street elements should be designed to encourage slow vehicle speeds and to signify the shared court’s intended use by pedestrians as well as vehicles.
See Figure 4.
Access from a shared court is limited to ensure low traffic volumes that can allow a safe mixing of pedestrians and vehicles.
Shared courts are limited to zones intended for more intense development to facilitate efficient use of land while preserving the landscape-intensive character of lower-density zones.
The following standards apply to shared courts: [ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4782; April 2020]

13.Landscape buffers. [ORD 4879; April 2026]
A.Purpose: Landscape buffers are intended to address potential visual or noise impacts at property lines where land uses or intensities are different on certain property line boundaries.
The buffers include spatial distance, landscaping, screening, and noise attenuation features as appropriate to address or mitigate the anticipated impact or impacts.[ORD 4879; April 2026]
B.Applicability: All new development and redevelopment subject to Design Review that meets the criteria below shall comply with the landscape buffering requirements of this Subsection.
1. All new development subject to Design Review shall comply with the landscape buffering requirements
2. Redevelopment of an existing site shall be required to meet applicable landscape buffer standards when the redevelopment is subject to Design Review (Section 40.20) and meets one or more of the following criteria:
a. Site modifications to facilitate outdoor activities related to site uses:
i. At least 1,000 square feet of site modifications are proposed within 30 linear feet of a property line subject to a landscape buffer standard.
ii. Site modifications for the purposes of this standard means pavement installation, the development of buildings and structures, or other physical changes.
iii.  When calculating area, all site modifications shall be considered within 30 feet if any part of the site modifications are within 30 feet of the property line subject to a landscape buffer standard.
b. New buildings or building additions:
i. At least 2,500 square feet of new gross floor area is proposed within 30 linear feet of a property line subject to a landscape buffer standard.
ii. When calculating area, all additional gross floor area shall be considered within 30 feet if any part of the new floor area is within 30 feet of a property line subject to a landscape buffer standard.
c. Any of the following site or building additions or expansions associated with an Industrial use or a Type A Conditional Use are proposed within 50 feet of a property line subject to a landscape buffer standard.
The site or building additions or expansions shall be considered within 50 feet if any portion of them is within 50 feet.
i. Additions of gross floor area, including accessory structures;
ii. New vehicular parking or maneuvering area;
iii. New loading area;
iv. New outdoor storage or staging area; or
v. Any other new outdoor area to support the use.
For example, a new sport court or bleachers on an existing Recreational Facility site.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
C.Exemptions:
1. A landscape buffer shall not be required along property lines that abut public or private streets unless otherwise required in Section 60.05.25.13.
2. A landscape buffer shall not be required along shared property lines that are under common ownership at the time of application submittal.
3. Natural Area Exemptions: A landscape buffer shall not be required along property lines, or portions thereof, with:
a. A Natural Area with a minimum width of 20 feet;
b. A Natural Area with a minimum width of 10 feet within which vegetation exists or is proposed that meets or exceeds the minimum tree and shrub planting size and density requirements of the applicable landscape buffer type; or
c. For sites abutting the Cooper Mountain Nature Park, a Natural Area exists on the site that meets or exceeds the width and planting requirements (existing and proposed plants) of the native plantings buffer.
d. For the purpose of this exemption, Resource Overlay land must be protected by a tract or easement to count as a Natural Area.
4. This Subsection does not apply to the development of small-scale commercial uses in the CM-RM zoning district that are subject to the applicable requirements of Section 60.05.60.
5. A landscape buffer shall not be required for the development or redevelopment of Public Parks that area Permitted Use in the underlying zoning district.
6. The following uses shall not be required to provide buffers if they are not part of a Multiple Use Development:
a. Public Sewer and Water and Utility Transmission Lines;
b. Railroad Tracks and Facilities;
c. Community Gardens;
d. Emergency Shelters; and
e. Cemetery [ORD 4879; April 2026]
D.Standards:
1. Developments shall meet the minimum landscape buffer requirements of Table 60.05.25.13.D.
2. Where required, the landscape buffer shall extend the length of all side and rear property lines except in the following areas:
a. Areas where emergency access is required;
b. Areas where a public utility easement exists.
This exemption only applies to trees and does not exempt the requirement of shrubs and ground cover; and
c. Areas required for visual or physical access purposes as determined by the City Traffic Engineer, City Police, or Fire District.
This exemption only applies to trees and shrubs and does not exempt the requirement of ground cover.
3. Buffers shall be applied to a development site consistent with the following:
a. If a development proposes multiple land uses on a site and Table 60.05.25.13.D would require more than one landscape buffer type and or width on the site, the applicant shall provide the buffer requirement with the greatest width, planting, and noise attenuation requirements along the entire property line(s), rather than multiple different landscape buffers along the property line.
b. If a proposed development site with only one use abuts multiple properties and Table 60.05.25.13.D would require more than one landscape buffer configuration, the applicant shall either:
i. Provide the landscape buffer configuration required by Table 60.05.25.13.D with the greatest width, planting, and noise attenuation along the entire lot line(s) where buffers are required; or
ii. Provide the Table 60.05.25.13.D-required landscape buffer configurations that correspond to each abutting property along the lot line(s).
iii. Option D.3.b.ii above shall not be used when the proposed use is a Type A Conditional Use.
c. Where a site proposed for development abuts vacant property located outside City limits, the buffer requirement shall be determined based on the equivalent zoning district of the abutting property as identified in Table 1, Section 1.5.2. of the Comprehensive Plan or a zone similar to the County zone as determined by the Director.
d. The following elements are allowed within the buffer in addition to the required elements:
Gardens, pedestrian pathways, vegetated stormwater facilities, arbors, trellises, fountains, ponds.
Mechanical equipment shall not be placed within the buffer.
[ORD 4879; April 2026] E.Landscape buffer types:
1.Partial screen (PB): The following plant types, sizes, and spacing are required for a partial screen buffer:
a. Trees: One tree is required per 30 linear feet of the required buffer area where the required buffer width is 10 feet wide or larger.
Evergreen trees shall be planted at a minimum height of 5 feet; deciduous trees shall be planted at a minimum 2-inch caliper.
At least 30 percent of the required trees in the buffer area shall be evergreen.
Spacing between trees may vary within the buffer area but shall not exceed 50 linear feet between trees;
b. Shrubs: One shrub with a minimum mature height of 4 feet is required for every 200 square feet of required buffer area.
At least 50 percent of the required shrubs in the buffer area shall be of a hedging, evergreen variety.
A minimum 3-foot-high wooden fence, masonry wall, or berm may be substituted for the required shrubs; and
c. Ground cover: Live ground cover consisting of low-height plants, shrubs, or grasses shall be planted in the remaining required buffer area.
Bare gravel, rock, bark or other similar materials may be used but shall be limited to no more than 25 percent of the required buffer area. 2.Full screen (FB): The following plant types, sizes, and spacing are required for a full screen buffer:
a. Trees: One tree is required per 30 linear feet of the required buffer area where the required buffer width is 10 feet wide or larger.
Evergreen trees shall be planted at a minimum height of 5 feet; deciduous trees shall be planted at a minimum 2-inch caliper.
At least 50 percent of the required trees in the buffer area shall be evergreen.
Spacing between trees may vary within the buffer area but shall not exceed 40 linear feet between trees;
b. Hedge or Fence: A continuous hedge of evergreen shrubs shall be planted to provide complete visual separation from the adjacent property within 2 years of planting.
The hedge shall be planted with a minimum height of 4 feet and a minimum opacity of 50 percent.
The hedge shall consist of species that will grow to a minimum mature height of 6 feet and a minimum opacity of 95 percent within 2 years.
A minimum 6-foot-high wooden fence, masonry wall, or berm that provides complete visual separation from the adjacent property may be substituted for the hedge.
When a fence, wall, or berm is used, trees and ground cover may be planted on either side of the structure or berm; and
c. Ground cover: Live ground cover consisting of low-height plants, shrubs, or grasses shall be planted in the remaining required buffer area.
Bare gravel, rock, bark, or other similar materials may be used but shall be limited to no more than 25 percent of the required buffer area.
d. In locations where a buffer is required across a street, the required buffer width requirement shall be 5 feet and the required hedge, fence, or wall shall be at least 5 feet from the right of way.
The required buffer width and required landscaping shall be between the street and the required hedge, fence, or wall.
3.Full screen with noise attenuation (FBN): Tree and ground cover requirements of the full screen buffer as described in Section 60.05.25.13.E.2.a and c. shall be met in addition to a minimum 6-foot-tall, solid, continuous masonry wall that provides noise attenuation and complete visual separation from the adjacent property.
Plantings may be located on either side of the masonry wall.
4.Native plantings buffer (NPB): This buffer applies to property lines that abut the Cooper Mountain Nature Park and provides a 25-foot transition of native plantings to protect the significant habitat within the Nature Park.
The following plant types, sizes, and spacing are required for the native plantings buffer:
a. All plantings within the required buffer area shall be native species.
Nuisance species shall be removed from the buffer area.
b. Trees: One tree is required per 30 linear feet of the required buffer area.
Evergreen trees shall be planted at a minimum height of 5 feet; deciduous trees shall be planted at a minimum 2-inch
caliper. At least 50 percent of the required trees in the buffer area shall be evergreen.
Spacing between trees may vary within the buffer area but shall not exceed 50 linear feet between trees.
c. Shrubs: One shrub with a minimum mature height of 4 feet is required for every 200 square feet of required buffer area.
At least 50 percent of the required shrubs in the buffer area shall be evergreen.
d. Ground cover: Live ground cover consisting of low-height plants, shrubs, or grasses shall be planted in the remaining required buffer area.
Bare gravel, rock, bark or other similar materials may be used but shall be limited to no more than 25 percent of the required buffer area.

F.Conditional Use categories.
To identify the applicable landscape buffer requirement in Subsection C.5 below, Conditional land uses are separated into the following categories based on the potential noise, visual, light, and glare impacts associated with such uses:
1.Type A Conditional Uses: Uses in this category require mitigation of both visual and noise impacts to minimize negative effects on the livability or appropriate use of adjacent or nearby properties.
The following land uses, when Conditional in the underlying zoning district, are Type A Conditional Uses in addition to any other land use as identified by the decision-making authority pursuant to Section 40.15:
a. Animal Care, Major, within 50 feet of a property line subject to a buffer requirement.
b. Commercial Amusement with any outdoor component intended for spectator amusement
c. Meeting Facilities with any outdoor component intended for meetings
d. Social Organizations with any outdoor social meeting component that can accommodate more than 50 people that is within 50 feet of a property line subject to a buffer requirement
e. Vehicles: Automotive Service, Major
f. Recreational Facilities with any outdoor recreation component, except facilities within a Public Park, within 50 feet of a property line subject to the buffer requirement.
g. Any other Conditional land use identified by the decision-making authority pursuant to the applicable approval criteria of Section 40.15 which warrants a noise-attenuating buffering to mitigate noise impacts.
2.Type B Conditional Uses: Type B Conditional land uses are those that require mitigation of visual impacts to minimize negative effects on the livability or appropriate use of adjacent or nearby properties.
Little to no noise impacts are anticipated with the operation of Type B Conditional Uses.
All Conditional Uses, except for Planned Unit Developments, that are not identified as Type A by Subsection C.4.a are Type B. For Planned Unit Developments, the uses within the development shall be evaluated for the appropriate buffer consistent with this Section.

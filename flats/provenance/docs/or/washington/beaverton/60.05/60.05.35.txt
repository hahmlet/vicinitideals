60.05.35. Building Design and Orientation Guidelines.

All guidelines apply in all zoning districts unless otherwise noted in Section 60.05.11 or within the guidelines below.
[ORD 4859; January 2025]
1.Building articulation and variety.
[ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Residential buildings should be of a limited length in order to avoid undifferentiated building elevations, reduce the mass of individual buildings, and create a scale of development that is pedestrian friendly and allow circulation between buildings by pedestrians.
(Standard 60.05.15.1.A)
B. Building elevations should be varied and articulated to provide visual interest to pedestrians.
Within larger projects, variations in architectural elements such as: building elevations, roof levels, architectural features, and exterior finishes should be provided.
(Standards 60.05.15.1.A and B)
C. To balance horizontal features on longer building elevations, vertical building elements, such as building entries, should be emphasized.
(Standard 60.05.15.1.B)
D. Buildings should promote and enhance a comfortable pedestrian scale and orientation.
This guideline does not apply to buildings in Industrial districts where the principal use of the building is manufacturing, assembly, fabricating, processing, packing, storage, wholesale or distribution activities.
(Standard 60.05.15.1.B) [ORD 4531; April 2010]
E. Building elevations entirely or partially visible from and within 200 feet of an adjacent street or major parking area should be articulated with architectural features such as windows, dormers, off-setting walls, alcoves, balconies or bays, or by other design features that reflect the building's structural system.
Undifferentiated blank walls facing a street, common green, shared court or major parking area should be avoided.
(Standards 60.05.15.1.B, C and D) [ORD 4542; June 2010] [ORD 4857; October 2024]
F. Building elevations entirely or partially visible from and within 100 feet of an adjacent street where the principal use of the building is manufacturing, assembly, fabricating, processing, packing, storage and wholesale and distribution activities in an Industrial zoning district, should be articulated with architectural features such as windows, dormers, off-setting walls, alcoves, balconies or bays, or by other design features that reflect the building's structural system.
Undifferentiated blank walls facing a street should be avoided.
(Standards 60.05.15.1.B and C) [ORD 4857; October 2024] [ORD 4859; January 2025] 2.Roofs and parapets.
[ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
A. Flat or low slope roofs should include a roofline that provides visual interest and establish a such as cornice treatments.
(Standard 60.05.15.2.A) [ORD 4782; April 2020] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Roofs on new buildings larger than 20,000 square feet in total floor area shall include sustainability features while allowing other rooftop uses essential to the building function and tenant needs.
(Standard 60.05.15.2.B) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
3.Building entrances.
A. All buildings should be oriented toward and accessed from the street.
Entries on streets should be more prominent than those facing parking lots.
Primary entrances should provide weather protection and should incorporate changes in mass, surface, or finish to emphasize the entrance.
On corner lots where neither street is a local street, the design of buildings at the intersection of two such streets shall provide an entry at or near the corner unless site constraints, physical constraints associated with the building, or safety concerns make a corner entry infeasible.
(Standard 60.05.15.3.A) [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Special attention should be given to designing primary building entrances that are both attractive and functional.
Primary entrances should provide weather protection and should incorporate changes in mass, surface, or finish to emphasize the entrance.
Shared Entrances should be made visually prominent and receive architectural emphasis.
(Standard 60.05.15.3.B) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026] 4.Exterior building materials.
A. Exterior building materials and finishes should convey an impression of permanence and durability.
Materials such as masonry, stone, wood, terra cotta, and tile are encouraged.
Windows are also encouraged, where they allow views to interior activity areas or displays.
(Standards 60.05.15.5)
B. Where masonry is used, decorative patterns (other than running bond pattern) should be provided, especially at entrances, building corners and at the pedestrian level.
These decorative patterns may include multi-colored masonry units, such as brick, tile, stone, or cast stone, in a layered or geometric pattern, or multi-colored ceramic tile bands used in conjunction with materials such as concrete.
This guideline does not apply to development in Industrial zones, where masonry is used for exterior finishes.
(Standards 60.05.15.5) [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
5.Roof-mounted equipment. All roof- and wall-mounted mechanical, electrical, communications, and service equipment should be screened from view from adjacent public streets by the use of parapets, walls, fences, enclosures, dense evergreen foliage, or by other suitable means.
(Standards 60.05.15.6.A through C) [ORD 4857;

6.Building location and orientation along streets in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
[ORD 4584; June 2012] [ORD 4706; May 2017] [ORD 4857; October 2024]
A. Buildings should be oriented toward and located within close proximity to public streets and public street intersections to provide a street wall, pedestrian interest, and convenient access for pedestrians.
If a building has frontage on more than one street, it should be oriented and designed to provide reasonably direct pedestrian access along the most active street frontages.
Buildings should also address transit facilities.
(Standard 60.05.15.6.A) [ORD 4462; January 2008] [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4706; May 2017] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. In the CM-CS and CM-HDR zones, buildings should be oriented toward and located in close proximity to property line(s) across the street from a Public Park and abutting trails in the right of way, when present.
On primary public street frontages in the CM-CS and CM-HDR zoning districts, building entrances should be oriented to streets, or have reasonably direct pedestrian connections to streets and pedestrian and transit facilities.
(Standard 60.05.15.6.B) [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. Parking beside building and located near the street near should be minimized.
(Standard 60.05.15.6.C) [ORD 4365; October 2005] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. Parking located at the street frontage on the side of a building should be minimized.
(Standard 60.05.15.7.D) [ORD 4706; May 2017] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
E. Site landscaping near the street shall be planted to fully cover areas not occupied by structures, paving, or hardscaped areas consistent with 60.05.25 or 60.05.45 (Standard 60.05.15.6.D) [ORD 4879; April 2026] 7.Height Transition.
A. Development on lots abutting property zoned RMA, RMB, or RMC, or a comparable Washington County zone shall be stepped back to reduce the visual and solar impact on neighboring residentially zoned lots.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
8.Ground floor elevations in Commercial and Multiple Use zones.
[ORD 4857; October 2024]
A. Ground-floor building elevations should be pedestrian oriented and treated with windows or glass doorway openings to the extent possible and where appropriate to the design and use of the building.
This guideline particularly applies to ground-floor building elevations situated along Neighborhood Route, Collector, Arterial, or Principal Arterial streets or along primary public street frontages in the CM-CS or CM-HDR zoning districts.
(Standard 60.05.15.8.A.) [ORD 4531; April 2010] [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
B. Where structured parking is adjacent to a street, the street-facing facades shall be sufficiently screened to minimize visual impacts to pedestrians and prevent light trespass.
(Standard 60.05.15.8.B) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
C. Except buildings used exclusively for residential use, ground-floor elevations shall provide weather protection along streets and plazas.
(Standard 60.05.15.8.C) [ORD 4879; April 2026]
1. Weather protection shall be architecturally integrated to reflect the pattern of store fronts and upper story windows/articulation.
Particular attention should be paid to primary building entrances; portions of buildings adjacent to bus stops and signalized intersections; areas above store fronts and display windows; and other areas where significant pedestrian waiting or browsing occurs.
This guideline does not apply in the CM-S or CM-HDR zoning districts.
[ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
2. Awnings and canopies shall balance weather protection with daylight penetration.
Continuous opaque canopies that run the full length of facades should be avoided.
[ORD 4879; April 2026]
D. For buildings taller than 30 feet, measured from grade plane to eave or top of parapet, with ground-floor commercial uses, building elevations facing the right of way, any internal drive, or any internal accessway should be designed with a base that establishes depth and visual interest, is visually distinctive, is proportional to the scale of the building, and is integrated into the building design.
(Standard 60.05.15.8.D) [ORD 4859; January 2025] [ORD 4879; April 2026]
9.Compact Detached Housing design.
[ORD 4584; June 2012]
A. Building elevations facing streets, shared courts and common greens should include pedestrian oriented design elements and other design features that provide articulation, variety, interest and quality.
(Standards 60.05.15.9. A, B, C, G, H, I, J, K, and L) [ORD 4576; January 2012]
B. [ORD 4576; January 2012] Alleys and shared courts are the preferred option to serve garages, and should be provided on all lots except where topography or other identified physical constraints preclude their use.
(Standards 60.05.15.9.D and K)
C. Garage openings should not be a dominant feature within shared courts.
(Standards 60.05.15.9. D, E, and
F) [ORD 4576; January 2012]
D. [ORD 4576; January 2012] The impact of curb cuts and driveways along shared courts and streets should be minimized.
(Standard 60.05.15.9.E)

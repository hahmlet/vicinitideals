tree-growth

WHY: Portland's Johnson Creek rule (City Code 33.537.125) holds back building where a tree 6 inches or more in trunk diameter (DBH) would have to be removed; a tree of 6 to 12 inches may go if replaced two-for-one, so the trees that truly block are those over 12 inches DBH. Our only tree data are heights from airborne laser scans: Metro "Canopy 2019" (summer 2019) and DOGAMI's canopy height model (flights 2004-2014 in outer SE Portland). The owner asks: is 7 to 12+ years long enough that ANY tree visible in those scans -- any height, any species -- is now over 12 inches DBH? If yes, any tree cover in the scan can simply be treated as a blocking tree; if no, we need to know which cases fail.

QUESTIONS (answer each with sources):
1. Annual trunk-diameter (DBH) growth for urban/residential trees in the Portland / Pacific Northwest maritime climate, per species, for the species common on Portland residential lots: Douglas-fir, western redcedar, bigleaf maple, Oregon white oak, red alder, black cottonwood, Norway maple, red maple, sweetgum, flowering cherry/plum, birch, Japanese maple, dogwood, holly, arborvitae/hedge conifers. Best source: the USDA Forest Service Urban Tree Database (McPherson, van Doorn, Peper 2016, General Technical Report PSW-GTR-253; its Pacific Northwest climate region and reference city) -- growth equations of DBH by age and height by DBH. Give inches/year ranges and the source table/page.
2. Using those equations: for a tree just tall enough to show up as canopy in a laser scan (state what height threshold Metro's Canopy 2019 or similar canopy products use -- often 6 to 15 ft -- SEEN or unknown), what is its likely DBH at scan time, and its DBH 7 years and 12 years later, per species? Which species / starting sizes would still be UNDER 12 inches after 7 years? After 12 years?
3. Small ornamentals that may never reach 12 inches at maturity (Japanese maple, dogwood, small flowering trees, hedges): list them with mature DBH ranges.
4. The other direction: could a tree too small to appear in a 2019 scan (a planted sapling, 1-2 inch caliper) reach 6 inches or 12 inches DBH by 2026? Which fast species?

ANSWER FORM (under 600 words): a table per question 1-2 (species | DBH growth in/yr | DBH at scan | +7 yr | +12 yr | source), then short answers to 3 and 4, then "BOTTOM LINE:" two sentences answering the owner's question, then "LOOKED:". Mark every number SEEN (with source) or INFERRED (with the arithmetic).
